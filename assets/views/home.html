One editing session on Furnace Hill Coffee

Five steps from the real editor and the published site, captured on 20 October 2026. Choose a step, or focus the list and use the arrow keys.

Step 1. Click the featured heading, choose Edit text, change the words, then press Done.

Step 2. Click the photo, choose Replace image and pick a file. Pagehaven fits it to the old frame and asks you to describe the new picture.

Step 3. Click “Read the post”, choose Link and pick About from the pages of this website.

Step 4. Publish lists every change before anything goes out. Your first publish makes the whole website public, every page, not only the one you edited.

Step 5. The published home page shows the new heading and photo at an address made from the site’s name, such as furnace-hill-coffee.{{ sites_domain }}.

What you can change by clicking

Nothing goes live until you press Publish. Until then every change is a draft only you see, and Undo still works when you come back tomorrow.

Words and headings
Change any text written in your pages, right where it appears. Make a word bold or italic, or turn it into a link.
Photos
Replace a picture with a PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or AVIF up to 8 MB, and write its description.
Links
Point a link or button at another page by picking its title, or at a web address, email or phone number.
Colour and spacing
Text and background colour, font size and weight, alignment, padding and margins, from a fixed set of controls.
Sections and pages
Hide, duplicate, move or remove a section. Add a page by copying one, or rename a page and its links follow.
Versions
Each publish is a numbered version. Make an earlier one live again, or start editing from it.

Text that your site’s own JavaScript creates in the browser, form fields, embeds and iframes stay as they are.

HTML pages with their pictures, styles and fonts

A page saved from an AI chat, an HTML template that ships finished pages, or the built folder of a static site. Pagehaven looks for index.html at the top of the ZIP, inside one folder, or in dist or build.

Imports, edits only part

Sites whose pages are built in the browser by JavaScript

A built React or Vite site imports and publishes. You can edit only the text and pictures written in its HTML files, and Pagehaven warns you when you import it.

Refused

Project code that still needs a build step

A folder of source files, such as package.json and src, with no finished pages. Downloads from Lovable, Bolt or v0 are usually this kind of project. Their built output imports, with the limits above.

After you create an account, Pagehaven checks the ZIP in your browser before it uploads and tells you if it holds source code.
